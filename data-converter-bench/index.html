Data Converter Bench

Topic 3 · slides 54–55

Two converters, one formula. Part 1 is a D/A you set and drive. Part 2 is a ramp A/D built from that same D/A. Everything on this page is computed from Vref ÷ 2n.

01

Predict, then run

8 minutes · no AI
  1. Screens down. On paper, for 8 bits on a 5 V reference: how many levels, the step size, the output at code 128, and the output at full scale.
  2. Still on paper: change the reference to 10 V. Does the step go up, down or stay — and by how much?
  3. Back to 5 V, now 12 bits. Same question.
  4. How many bits would you need to resolve 1 mV on a 5 V reference?
Then open the bench and check all four. Mark each one right or wrong. The wrong ones are the useful ones — write one line on what you had wrong.
02

Make AI commit

10 minutes · AI never gives the answer

Paste this, exactly as written:

I have a simulator of an 8-bit D/A
converter with an adjustable reference.
Make FIVE specific, numerical, testable
claims about what it will show when the
reference is 7.5 V. Each claim must be a
single number or a yes/no. Number them
1 to 5. Do not explain. Do not hedge.
Set the bench to 8 bits, 7.5 V and test every claim against it. Mark each true or false. Report your score out of five — and for each false one, say what AI got wrong.
03

Settle it

9 minutes · together, then alone
  • Scores on the board. How many of five did AI get right?
  • One question for the room: why is full scale 4.9805 V and not 5.000 V?
  • You must measure 0–5 V to the nearest millivolt. How many bits, which ladder, and why?
Finish these two, in writing:
• Before today I thought a converter at its highest code would output …
• AI got __ of 5 right. The one it got wrong was wrong because …

Part 2 · A/D — ramp (counter) type

clock it yourself, one pulse at a time

A ramp A/D is the D/A above, plus a comparator and a counter. Press START to sample and hold the input and reset the counter, then press CLK to advance it one count at a time. Watch the D/A output climb, and watch the comparator flip the instant it reaches the held input — that count is the answer.

Each clock pulse adds one step. The conversion ends the moment the staircase reaches the dashed line.

Output is the ideal converter output: Vout = code × Vref ÷ 2ⁿ. Binary weighted branch resistors are Ri = 10 kΩ × 2^(n−1−i) with Rf = 5 kΩ, so the MSB contributes half of full scale and the LSB contributes one step. MSB tolerance is the error that would cost half a step: 1 ÷ 2ⁿ.
