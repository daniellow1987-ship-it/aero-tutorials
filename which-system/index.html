Topic 10 · Electronic Instruments & Digital Aircraft Systems

Nine real flight-deck events. For each one: identify the system responsible (EFIS, EICAS/ECAM, BITE, ACARS, FBW, FMS, TCAS, GPS or IRS), then the specific detail within it the topic actually distinguishes. Every verdict traces to a stated fact.

01

Predict, then check

8 minutes · no AI
  1. Screens down. On paper, for cases 1, 2, 3 and 7 below: name the system and the specific detail, with one sentence why.
  2. Then open the identifier and check all four. Mark each right or wrong.
The wrong ones are the useful ones — write one line on what fact you were missing, not just "I was wrong".
02

Make AI commit

10 minutes · AI never gives the answer first

Paste this, exactly as written, about Case 5 (the flight-control case):

I am an aerospace electronics student.
Here is an aircraft event: [paste Case 5's
brief]. Make FIVE specific, testable
claims: the system responsible, the exact
detail within it, and three supporting
facts about why that detail fits. Number
them 1 to 5. Do not hedge.
Open Case 5 in the identifier and adjudicate every claim against it. Mark each true or false, and for each false one, say what AI got wrong.
03

Settle it

9 minutes · together, then alone
  • Scores on the board. How many of five did AI get right on Case 5?
  • One question for the room: why are EICAS and ECAM not two different systems?
  • Transfer (no identifier, justify from the summary table below): the flight crew gets an aural alert and a display command to climb immediately, because another aircraft is now 700 feet away vertically and 25 seconds away on present trajectory. System, detail, and why?
Finish these two, in writing:
• Before today I thought BITE only ran when an engineer started a test, because …
• AI got __ of 5 right on Case 5. The one it got wrong was wrong because …
